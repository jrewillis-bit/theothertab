<h1>I’m Investing £5 Per Day for a Year (Instead of Buying Coffee)</h1>For the next 365 days, I’m investing <strong>£5 every single day</strong>.

Not because I’m new to investing.

Not because I’ve suddenly “discovered” the stock market.

But because I wanted to redirect something small and forgettable — coffee money — into something intentional.

£5 used to disappear into flat whites and impulse café stops.

Now it goes automatically into my <strong>Trading 212 Stocks &amp; Shares ISA</strong>, invested in the <strong>Vanguard S&amp;P 500 UCITS ETF (Accumulating)</strong>.

No drama. No hype. Just automation.

<hr>

<h2>The Setup</h2>
<ul>
	<li><strong>Platform:</strong> Trading 212</li>
	<li><strong>Account:</strong> Stocks &amp; Shares ISA</li>
	<li><strong>Investment:</strong> Vanguard S&amp;P 500 UCITS ETF (Acc)</li>
	<li><strong>Amount:</strong> £5 per day</li>
	<li><strong>Method:</strong> AutoInvest (daily)</li>
</ul>That’s:

<ul>
	<li>£35 per week</li>
	<li>~£150 per month</li>
	<li><strong>£1,825 per year</strong></li>
</ul>Not life-changing.

But habits compound long before money does.

<hr>

<h2>Why the S&amp;P 500?</h2>I chose the accumulating version because dividends are automatically reinvested.

Simple. Automatic. Compounding.

It tracks 500 of the largest US companies.

It’s low-cost.

It’s broad (though US-focused).

Is it globally diversified? No.

Am I comfortable with that for this experiment? Yes.

<hr>

<h2>Why Daily Instead of Monthly?</h2>Mathematically, lump sum investing can outperform.

But this isn’t about optimisation.

It’s about identity.

£5 per day:

<ul>
	<li>Builds consistency</li>
	<li>Removes timing decisions</li>
	<li>Makes spending intentional</li>
</ul>Every time I consider buying coffee, I now think:

<blockquote>Is this worth skipping a day of investing?

</blockquote>Sometimes it is.

But now it’s conscious.

<hr>

<h2>Past Performance Isn’t Future Performance</h2>It’s easy to look at an S&amp;P 500 chart and assume it will continue climbing forever.

Historically, returns have been strong.

But:

<ul>
	<li>Markets can stagnate for years</li>
	<li>They can fall sharply</li>
	<li>Leadership shifts between regions and sectors</li>
</ul>The last decade favoured large US companies.

The next decade might not.

That’s why this isn’t about chasing returns.

It’s about building a durable habit.

<hr>

<h2>What If I Did This for 10 Years?</h2><strong>£5 per day</strong> = <strong>£1,825 per year</strong>

<strong>10 years</strong> = <strong>£18,250 invested</strong>

If (and it’s a big if) average returns looked like:

<ul>
	<li><strong>4% annually</strong> → ~£22,000</li>
	<li><strong>7% annually</strong> → ~£25,500</li>
	<li><strong>10% annually</strong> → ~£29,000</li>
</ul>These are hypothetical illustrations — not predictions.

But they show what consistency plus compounding can do over time.

Not flashy.

Just powerful.

<hr>

<h2>The Real Experiment</h2>The interesting part isn’t whether it becomes £22k or £29k.

It’s whether:

<ul>
	<li>I invest every day for 365 days</li>
	<li>I permanently shift how I see “small” spending</li>
	<li>I strengthen the identity of someone who invests consistently</li>
</ul>Because wealth is rarely built through dramatic moves.

It’s built through boring consistency.

<hr>

I’m sharing my personal approach and experience. What works for me may not be suitable for your financial situation, goals, or risk tolerance.

Investing involves risk. The value of investments can fall as well as rise, and you may get back less than you invest.

Always do your own research and consider speaking to a qualified financial adviser if unsure.

<hr>£5 feels insignificant when spent.

It feels intentional when invested.

One year from now, I’ll know whether coffee money turned into something more meaningful.

Not just financially.

Behaviourally.
