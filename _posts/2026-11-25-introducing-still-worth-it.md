Most reviews are written at the point when a product is at its most exciting. It has just come out of the box, everything is new and the next version hasn't yet appeared.

That's useful if you're deciding what to buy today. But it's not necessarily the information I want when I'm deciding whether something is worth keeping, repairing or buying second-hand.

What happens six months later? A year later? What about five years down the line?

That's the idea behind **Still Worth It?**, a new series on The Other Tab.

## The review that comes after the review

Rather than concentrating on launch-day specifications, I want to look at things after they've become part of everyday life. Things I've actually used, not products borrowed for an afternoon or bought simply to review.

Did they solve the problem I bought them for? Are they reliable? Have they become expensive to maintain? Are there things I wish I'd known before buying them?

Perhaps most importantly, would I spend my own money on them again today?

## Why I think this matters

There is always a new laptop, phone, car or gadget around the corner. It's easy to start thinking that the equipment we already own is somehow inadequate simply because a newer version exists.

I bought a second-hand ThinkPad for £235 and installed Linux Mint. I also use a UGREEN NAS to run services such as Jellyfin and AdGuard, back up photographs from my phone and sync data off-site. Both are interesting examples of technology chosen for a purpose rather than for the sake of having the latest thing.

But a positive first impression isn't the same as a long-term verdict. I want to revisit these purchases once I have enough experience to say something useful about how they've held up.

The same principle applies to things beyond computers. An older car, a camera or a household purchase can be worth revisiting, particularly when replacing it would cost considerably more than keeping it going.

## What each review will cover

I'll use a consistent set of questions, adapted to the product:

- **Ownership:** How long have I had it, what did I pay and what did I need it to do?
- **Everyday use:** How often do I actually use it, and does it still meet that need?
- **Reliability:** What has gone wrong, and what maintenance or repairs have been necessary?
- **True cost:** Where I have records, what has it cost to own and run beyond the initial price?
- **Limitations:** What frustrates me now that the novelty has worn off?
- **The verdict:** Would I keep it, buy it again, repair it or replace it?

I'll distinguish recorded costs from estimates, and say where I haven't measured something. There is little point pretending to have precise figures when I don't.

## A different kind of recommendation

Not everything will receive a positive verdict. Sometimes a purchase doesn't live up to expectations. Sometimes the right choice is to repair something, and sometimes replacing it really does make sense.

I don't want to turn keeping old things into a competition, either. The question is whether something continues to offer value in the context of how it is actually used.

There is something refreshing about a review that doesn't end by encouraging you to buy the next thing.

That's what I hope Still Worth It? can offer.
